# Baseline Results — Demand Forecasting

Generated by `scripts/train_demand_baseline.py` against
`data/processed/demand_daily.parquet` (33 routes with
>=200 daily observations, 26832 scoreable rows
after calendar-gap fill and lag computation).

Train: everything before each route's last 56 days.
Test: each route's last 56 days (1848 rows).

| Model | MAE | RMSE | MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naive last-week (t-7) | 39.38 | 84.44 | 1.265 |
| HistGradientBoostingRegressor | 33.71 | 62.39 | 1.164 |

MAE improvement over baseline: 14.4%.

**Verdict: the model beats the naive baseline by a meaningful margin — worth keeping.**

MAPE is high (>1.0) for both models — expected, not a bug: these 33
routes range from ~13 to ~2,841 daily passengers (median 49), and MAPE
blows up on the low-volume end (a miss of 3 on a true value of 1 is a
300% error). MAE/RMSE are the more meaningful metrics here given that
spread; a per-route-normalized or log-scale target would likely tame
MAPE further but wasn't needed to answer "does a learned model beat the
naive rule."

Model artifact: `models/demand/histgb_v1.joblib`.


# Baseline Results — Travel Time

Generated by `scripts/train_travel_time_baseline.py` against
`data/processed/legs.parquet` joined to `trips.parquet`
(373 (line, from_stop, to_stop) leg-groups with
>=30 occurrences, 170681 scoreable rows).

Train: departures before 2026-06-15. Test: the last 8
weeks (759 rows). 18 test rows had no matching
leg-group in train and used the train-wide mean as fallback.

| Model | MAE (s) | RMSE (s) | MAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical mean (line, from_stop, to_stop, hour, dow) | 93.9 | 209.6 | 0.324 |
| HistGradientBoostingRegressor | 86.9 | 184.0 | 0.371 |

MAE improvement over baseline: 7.4%.

**Verdict: the model does NOT clearly beat the historical-mean baseline — the mean rule is the better default here.**

Model artifact: `models/travel_time/histgb_v1.joblib`.
